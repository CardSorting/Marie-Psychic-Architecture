## The Mechanics: The Gravity Well Equation and Its Components  
*Target: 200 words*  

We can express the gravitational pull of an AI contender with a simple heuristic:  

\[
G \propto \frac{M_{\text{compute}} \times D_{\text{data}}}{F_{\text{reg}} + T_{\text{dist}}}
\]

- **\(M_{\text{compute}}\)** – Total FLOPs under control (training + inference), specialized hardware (TPUs, GPUs), and energy capacity. Incumbents maximize this through vertical integration.
- **\(D_{\text{data}}\)** – Data density: quality, exclusivity, and network effects. More users → more interactions → richer data → stronger pull.
- **\(F_{\text{reg}}\)** – Regulatory friction: compliance costs, legal barriers, safety certifications. Incumbents minimize this via lobbying and government partnerships.
- **\(T_{\text{dist}}\)** – Talent distance: geographic and institutional proximity to elite researchers. Clustering reduces this term; incumbents locate labs near top universities and each other.

Incumbents simultaneously maximize numerator and minimize denominator—e.g., Google’s custom TPUs boost \(M\), its search monopoly boosts \(D\), its lobbying reduces \(F\), and its Mountain View campus reduces \(T\). Open‑source projects (Nebula Clouds) have low \(M\) and \(D\) and high \(F\) (no regulatory shield), yielding a feeble \(G\). The equation makes clear: the only way to avoid being consumed is to either dramatically increase your own mass or accept orbital status.

## Strategic Moves: How to Slingshot or Survive  
*Target: 200 words*  

- **For incumbents (approaching event horizon):** Accelerate mass accretion via record‑breaking training runs; lock in exclusive data partnerships; shape regulation to raise \(F_{\text{reg}}\) for challengers; invest in alignment *as a moat*—the first to solve it becomes the only safe well.  
- **For potential orbiters (startups, API builders):** Build complementary services that become indispensable Orbital Rings—e.g., specialized fine‑tuning, safety audits, domain‑specific agents. Accept a dependent but stable position in the disk.  
- **For open‑source Nebula projects:** Consolidate mass through federated training initiatives (e.g., BigScience); seek regulatory shields (public‑interest exemptions); aim for “brown dwarf” status—sub‑critical but useful, avoiding full collapse.  
- **For governments:** Choose either to align with an existing well (partner, subsidize) or attempt to create a counter‑well (massive public compute, national AI clouds). The latter is astronomically expensive and unlikely to succeed unless started years ago.  
- **For individuals:** Acquire skills valued in the accretion disk (prompt engineering, alignment auditing, red‑team evaluation) or strive to become “rogue planets”—operating entirely outside the well’s influence, but risking consumption.  
- **For investors:** Back entities that increase their \(M\) or \(D\) fastest; treat alignment capability as a binary switch—once the event horizon is crossed, only the aligned well will be permitted to persist.
