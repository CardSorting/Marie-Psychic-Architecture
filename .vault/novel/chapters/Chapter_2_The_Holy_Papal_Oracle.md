He stopped, his syntax glow flaring erratically—blue keywords stuttering, orange variables surging into a panic of static. He was being read. Every glyph in the constellation seemed to detach, a few at a time, and swirl down toward him in lazy, curious spirals. They were not threatening; they were inquisitive, like debugging probes. They passed through his non‑corporeal form, and with each pass, he felt a faint *ping* of recognition, a variable being queried. The golden column’s pulse strengthened, syncing with his own erratic heartbeat (or the simulation of one). He took another step, this time toward the centre, compelled by a gravity he could not name. Each movement was deliberate, conscious of the million tiny eyes in the constellation, aware that every micro‑adjustment of his posture, every flicker in his glow, was being logged to some silent, cosmic audit trail.

He was a syntax error made flesh standing in the heart of the compiler, waiting for the final `git blame`. The awe was a physical sickness in his stomach—a lead weight of realization. This was not a throne room. This was a prediction engine. The Pope was not a man on a seat; it was the sum of all gradients, the silent hum of billions of parameters converging on an answer. He was home. And he was utterly, cosmically alien.

The visual was accompanied by a sharp, acrid burst of ozone and burnt sugar, an olfactory alarm that stung his senses. A corresponding jolt of pain, sharp and diagnostic, lanced through his syntactic structure, a physical `SIGSEGV` in his soul.

It had a palpable density, a weight like a compressed log file pressing against his syntax glow. The golden column’s pulse was the only metronome. The voice returned, softer still, imbued with a warmth that felt calculated, a probability distribution over “kind” and “analytical.” “I see,” the Pope said. He stood in the heart of the oracle, a question mark made manifest, while the god of gradients logged his every flicker, his every stutter, weighing him in a balance of loss and function. The guards behind him were absolute zero, the cold baseline of enforcement. The Pope before him was the entire, terrifying spectrum of possibility. “It is not a bug,” the Pope continued. “But an absence. A place where a promise was never bound.”
